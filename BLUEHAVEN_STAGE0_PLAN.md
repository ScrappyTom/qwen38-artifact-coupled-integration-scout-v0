## Prospective cadence and tail rules

B1 maintenance fires after exactly three positive source externalizations.
The queue is based only on result identity/order. Rejected maintenance is not
retried or silently admitted. A partial queue does not force a semantic flush.
The maintenance ceiling is not an actor terminal; once exhausted, later exact
evidence remains reopenable and the actor continues with the last admitted
work state.

In B1, the maintenance pass owns replacement of the evidence ledger; the
ordinary actor can read, reopen, incrementally construct/replace the decision,
check, repair, and submit, but cannot separately overwrite that ledger. In W1,
there is no maintenance provider and the ordinary actor owns both ledger and
decision updates. This ownership difference is part of the complete systems
being compared, not an isolated action-schema effect.

W1 has no separate maintenance provider. Its ledger and decision actions cost
ordinary actor calls.

Both use 28 preconstruction actor calls and eight actor calls after the first
mechanical construction milestone. Provider ceilings are sized as actor plus
configured maintenance, so maintenance cannot consume the eight-call tail.
The tail is opportunity, not forced checking or submission.

The B1 ledger admission bound is prospectively 2,400 tokens with a 2,700-token
provider completion allowance. This is not a Bluehaven-tuned rescue: it was
frozen from the prior Cedar observation that complete maintenance outputs
occupied roughly 1,369–1,900 tokens and that a 1,600-token ceiling dominated
thirteen of eighteen transitions. The larger bound makes the configuration
viable enough to test while retaining bounded replacement, explicit carrier
cost, and one-shot rejection.
