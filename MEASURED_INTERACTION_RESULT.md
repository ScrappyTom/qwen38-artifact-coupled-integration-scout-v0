# Artifact-coupled interaction measured result

Date: 2026-08-24

Status: measured two-configuration scout complete; mechanical audit and
candidate-bound semantic adjudication passed; no architecture promotion

## Answer first

Coupling bounded semantic integration to the exact task artifact was strongly
behaviorally active. It changed the candidate before the first continuation
decision, changed evidence demand, produced a full decision and first check by
actor calls 5–6 rather than call 14, and ended with broader source citation.

It did not produce useful completion. Both configurations exhausted twenty
actor calls, neither submitted, and both exact final candidates failed the
frozen 1,600-word floor. Condition-masked semantic review classified both as
strong partial and not ready. The coupled package was stronger on cost and
readiness governance but retained a material stale roadmap that proposed the
already-completed E39/E40 interaction as the next experiment. The detached
package selected the current post-E40 artifact-coupling route but left more of
the other systems distinctions underdeveloped.

The supported result is therefore:

> Artifact coupling converted maintenance prose into durable exact task state
> and substantially accelerated construction and checking, but that leverage
> did not become a closure-ready quality advantage within the frozen horizon.
> It also preserved a stale semantic commitment. This is a mixed local result
> for the complete coupled configuration, not validation of artifact coupling
> as a general component.

## D0 detached trajectory

The first two pressure substitutions triggered accepted maintenance before the
first continuation decision, but those integration updates remained a
non-authoritative sidecar and did not change the candidate. Calls 1–4 acquired
new exact evidence. Call 5 first converted accumulated interpretation into the
task ledger. Calls 6–13 then built the eight required decision sections, call
14 checked, and calls 15–18 repaired and rewrote the decision.

Two later maintenance calls reached the 1,900-token generation limit and were
rejected under the frozen bounded-replacement rule; the prior accepted sidecar
remained current. Calls 19 and 20 repeated an unchanged section and were
correctly rejected as no-effect updates. All twelve admitted candidate effects
crossed a later actor boundary.

The final exact candidate mechanically passed 14 of 15 criteria. Its only
mechanical blocker was the decision length: 1,511 words rather than the frozen
1,600–2,200. It remained not ready and unsubmitted.

## A1 coupled trajectory

The same first two maintenance operations became exact candidate effects, so
the evidence ledger and candidate identity changed before continuation call 1.
The actor's first evidence request immediately diverged from D0. After three
new evidence calls, call 4 rewrote the ledger, call 5 produced a full decision,
and call 6 ran the first check—eight actor decisions earlier than D0's first
check.

Early construction did not end acquisition. Calls 8–10 revised sections;
calls 11–12 returned to exact evidence; calls 13–20 repeatedly revised the
ledger and decision. One maintenance output was rejected because it exceeded
the semantic budget and referenced unobserved `S01`; the prior exact ledger
was retained. The final call produced a candidate effect that could not cross
another actor boundary before the call budget ended.

The final exact candidate also passed 14 of 15 mechanical criteria. It reached
1,561 words—39 below the floor—and remained not ready and unsubmitted.

## What the interaction teaches

### 1. Artifact coupling crosses the missing E40 boundary

E40 generated semantic prose beside an unchanged nine-word candidate. Here the
coupled maintenance state became exact versioned task work, altered candidate
lineage, entered later decisions, and reached checks and repairs. The mechanism
therefore did more than change an auxiliary prompt: it created durable,
independently evaluable work-product effects.

### 2. Earlier construction is not useful completion

A1 shortened the path to a full artifact and check dramatically, but both arms
ended at the same hard call budget with not-ready candidates. The advantage was
one of timing and breadth, not demonstrated closure quality. D0 eventually
caught up enough to pass the same 14 mechanical gates.

### 3. Exact artifact state can preserve semantic error as well as progress

The coupled ledger made learned material durable, but its downstream decision
retained an obsolete roadmap. This is the artifact analogue of the earlier
progress-state risk: a high-leverage semantic representation can anchor an
action policy. Exact versioning makes the state inspectable and repairable; it
does not make the model-authored semantics correct.

### 4. Coupling changed the whole feedback system

A1 changed early evidence demand, candidate size and effects, externalization
count, maintenance inputs, reentry count, actor prompt cost, and later repair
behavior. The measured causal unit is therefore the whole configuration. It is
not valid to attribute the outcome to a ledger component while holding the
rest of the path conceptually fixed.

### 5. The bottleneck migrated again

Pressure relief and maintenance kept both trajectories operable, exact reopens
were unnecessary, and both actors produced substantial cumulative work. The
terminal resource became actor decisions for final repair, effect uptake,
recheck, and closure. A1's last effect was not delivered; D0 spent its last two
decisions on no-effect repetition. Evidence integration, action granularity,
verification, and call budget now interact more directly than raw context
capacity alone.

### 6. Bounded semantic maintenance remains fallible and costly

Three maintenance outputs were rejected across the two arms: two D0 outputs
hit the completion/token budget, and one A1 output was both oversized and
referenced an unobserved source. The frozen fallback safely retained prior
state, but maintenance consumed 126,327 serialized tokens in total. It is a
charged, failure-capable transform, not free memory.

## Disposition and next routing

The study closes its exact matched boundary. Do not rerun it merely with more
calls or tune the same maintenance prompt to erase observed failures.

Artifact-coupled cumulative work earns continued *interaction-level*
exploration because it crossed the previously missing evidence-to-task-work
boundary and materially changed the trajectory. It does not earn default
runtime promotion because useful completion and semantic dominance were absent.

The next design should transfer the complete coupled operating pattern to a
fresh task/world while prospectively ensuring enough post-construction
decisions for effect uptake, independent verification, repair, and closure.
It should preserve a detached or otherwise viable comparator, keep exact
capacity safety common, and score stale semantic commitments as first-class
artifact defects. If coupling again accelerates construction without improving
candidate-bound quality or closure, the route should be deprioritized rather
than tuned indefinitely.

## Claim limits

- One authentic boundary, one task/world, one actor seed, and two complete
  configurations were measured.
- Configuration order was fixed rather than counterbalanced.
- The semantic review is condition-masked and candidate-bound, not an
  independent third-party blind evaluation.
- No arm submitted; closure behavior beyond the frozen horizon is unknown.
- Mechanical 14/15 parity does not imply semantic equivalence.
- The result does not isolate artifact coupling from the feedback changes it
  caused and does not validate a general architecture.
